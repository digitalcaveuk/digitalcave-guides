Local search rewards businesses that are easy to verify, easy to find and easy to trust. This checklist covers the seven jobs that matter most for a Birmingham business, in the order we would do them. Most take an afternoon or less.

## 1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

This is the single biggest local factor you control. Follow Google's own guidance on [managing your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) and check each of these:

- The business name is the name customers actually know, with no keywords added.
- The primary category is the most specific one that fits, with a few secondary categories.
- Opening hours are correct, including bank holidays.
- Services are listed, with a short plain description of each.
- At least a handful of real photos: premises, team, finished work.

If you serve customers at their address and do not have a shopfront, set a service area instead of showing your address.

## 2. Keep your name, address and phone number the same everywhere

Search engines cross-check your business details. If your website says "Ltd", a directory says "Limited" and another still has an old phone number, trust drops. Pick one exact format and use it on your website, your Business Profile and every listing.

Write the format down in one place. When a team member or supplier asks for your details, they copy from that, not from memory.

## 3. Build a proper local landing page

A homepage that mentions Birmingham once is not a local page. Create one page for each main service you want to be found for in the city, and make each one genuinely useful:

- A clear heading that says what you do and where.
- Who the service is for, what is included and how it works.
- Local proof: named projects, areas you cover, accreditations.
- Answers to the questions customers ask before they call.
- One obvious next step, such as a call button or short form.

Keep one topic per page. Two services on one page usually means neither ranks well. For a worked example of how a local service page can be structured, see our [SEO company in Birmingham](https://digitalcave.uk/seo-company-birmingham) page.

## 4. Ask for reviews, and answer every one

Reviews affect both rankings and whether a searcher clicks on you. Ask every happy customer, soon after the job, with a direct link to your review page. Reply to every review, good or bad, calmly and specifically.

Never buy reviews, offer rewards for them or write your own. Google's [policy on prohibited and restricted content](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2622994) covers fake and incentivised reviews, and breaking it can get listings suspended.

## 5. Add citations that are accurate and useful

A citation is a mention of your business details on another site. A small number of accurate ones on reputable sites beats dozens of low-quality directories. Start with:

- The major mapping and search platforms.
- Industry bodies and trade associations you belong to.
- Local business groups and chambers of commerce.

Check each listing against your written format from step 2 before you submit.

## 6. Mark up your business with structured data

Add LocalBusiness schema to your website so search engines can read your details without guessing. Include the name, address, phone number, opening hours and the services you offer, and make sure it matches what is visible on the page. Test it before you publish.

## 7. Track what is happening in Search Console

Set up [Google Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9128668) and submit your sitemap. Then check it once a month:

- Which queries show your pages, and at what average position.
- Which pages get impressions but few clicks (usually a title or description problem).
- Whether any local pages are not indexed.

Pair it with Business Profile insights, which show calls, direction requests and website clicks, so you can see what the work is producing.

## A simple monthly routine

1. Check your Business Profile details and reply to new reviews.
2. Review Search Console for pages that moved up or down.
3. Add one useful piece of content or update one local page.
4. Ask for reviews from the month's customers.

Consistent, boring work done every month beats a big push once a year.
